02 · Regulate first

Lower the volume before choosing.

When activation rises, thinking can become rigid, rushed, foggy, or shut down. Regulation does not excuse harmful behavior; it helps you regain choice.

A

A · Anchor

Feel your feet, seat, and pelvis making contact with the floor or chair.

B

B · Breathe

Notice one complete breath. No need to force it deeper.

C

C · Connect

Place a hand where it feels supportive. Say: “I can take this one step at a time.”

Ready

A gentle longer exhale may help your body downshift. Stop if uncomfortable.

HOLD

Containment hold

  1. Place your right hand under your left armpit.
  2. Wrap your left hand around your upper right arm.
  3. Notice your body without forcing change.
  4. If it helps: “This is hard, and this moment will pass.”
Choice matters.

Skip touch-based practices if they feel uncomfortable. Orient to the room, walk, stretch, or sip water instead.

03 · Assess

Is the system out of balance?

Burnout is an occupational phenomenon associated with unmanaged chronic workplace stress—not a personal failure. Rate each work condition from 0 (working well) to 4 (serious strain).

Three systems shape the experience

Personal practices can support recovery, but they cannot substitute for relational repair and structural change.

IND

Individual system

Nervous system, health, needs, values, coping, and available choices.

Helpful: regulation and support
REL

Relational system

Power differences, communication, trust, conflict, feedback, and repair.

Helpful: boundaries and accountability
ORG

Organizational system

Workload, staffing, policy, incentives, equity, reporting channels, and culture.

Helpful: structural action

Your nervous system may move between states

Mobilized
fight / flight, urgency, racing thoughts
Regulated
flexible attention, connection, choice
Shut down
freeze, numbness, fog, withdrawal

These states are protective responses, not character flaws. Aim for enough steadiness—not perfect calm.

05 · De-catastrophize

Take the thought seriously—without treating it as a certainty.

Catastrophizing jumps from “something could go wrong” to “the worst will happen and I will not cope.” This practice separates possibility, probability, impact, and coping capacity.

ALARM

Alarm story

“The worst outcome is certain, permanent, and unmanageable.”

CHECK

Balanced forecast

“Several outcomes are possible. I can estimate likelihood, prepare, and respond step by step.”

06 · Choose a next step

You do not need to decide everything today.

Safety comes first. Choose the smallest step that increases clarity, support, or options.

Stabilize

Reduce exposure, take a break if possible, tend to sleep/food/medical needs, and speak with someone trustworthy.

Clarify & document

Review policy, preserve factual records lawfully, note dates/witnesses/impact, and seek qualified advice before high-stakes action.

Set a boundary

Make a specific, observable request. Decide what you will do if the behavior continues.

Build options

Name what keeps you in place, strengthen support, estimate financial runway, update materials, and exit professionally if safe.

What keeps me here?

Private incident log

Record observable facts and store information only where lawful and safe. Avoid taking confidential/proprietary material. This tool cannot determine what evidence is legal to retain.

Boundary language

Ask for clarity

“To make sure I understand, please confirm the priority, deadline, and what should be deprioritized.”

Name impact + request

“When feedback is delivered in front of the team, I have difficulty engaging productively. Please share performance feedback with me privately and include specific examples.”

Pause escalation

“I want to address this. I’m not able to continue while voices are raised. I can return to the conversation at [time] with [support person] present.”

Decline overreach

“I can complete A by Friday. If B is also urgent, which current task should move?”

Request written confirmation

“I’m sending a brief summary of what I understood: [decision, owner, deadline]. Please correct anything I have misunderstood.”

Respond to a last-minute demand

“I see that this is urgent. I can begin today, but completing it by [time] would require moving [current priority]. Please confirm which outcome you prefer.”

Decline personal questions

“I prefer to keep that private. I’m happy to discuss what you need from me for the work.”

Address repeated interruptions

“I’d like to finish my thought, and then I want to hear your response.”

Ask for specific feedback

“Please identify the specific behavior or work product that needs to change, the expected standard, and how progress will be evaluated.”

Bring support to a meeting

“Given the importance of this conversation, I would like [name/role] to attend and help us document next steps.”

End after-hours contact

“I am offline outside [hours]. I will review this during my next working period. If the role requires different coverage, let’s clarify that expectation and workload.”

Escalate through a formal channel

“I raised this concern on [dates], and the pattern has continued. I am requesting a formal review under [policy] and written confirmation of the next steps.”

Do not use a script if it may increase danger or retaliation. A confidential consultation may be the safer first move.

07 · Support & context

This is bigger than self-care.

Rest and grounding can help, but they cannot repair chronic understaffing, coercion, discrimination, unsafe conditions, or retaliation. Responsibility increases with power.

If there is immediate danger

Leave the area if you safely can and contact local emergency services. In the U.S., call or text 988 for free, confidential emotional crisis support; call 911 for immediate danger.

Know the limits of this tool

This is educational, not therapy, diagnosis, legal, HR, financial, or emergency advice. Employment rights vary by location and facts. Consult a licensed professional, union representative, employee-assistance program, or employment attorney as appropriate.
